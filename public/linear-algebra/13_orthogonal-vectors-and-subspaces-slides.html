Orthogonal Vectors and Subspaces

Orthogonal = perpendicular. In markets it means "has nothing to do with": market-neutral, uncorrelated.

xᵀy = 0correlation = cos θMIT 18.06, Lecture 14
The test

Dot product zero

$$ x \perp y \iff x^Ty = 0 $$

Why: Pythagoras. $\|x + y\|^2 = \|x\|^2 + \|y\|^2 + 2x^Ty$, so the right-triangle rule holds exactly when $x^Ty = 0$.

Zero-cost trade $(1, -1)$ ⊥ budget row $(1, 1)$: $1 - 1 = 0$.

In markets

Correlation is a cosine

Demeaned returns as vectors: Nifty $(-20, 0, 20)$, a strategy $(5, -10, 5)$.

$$ (-20)(5) + 0 + (20)(5) = 0 $$

Orthogonal = uncorrelated: a market-neutral strategy.

Nifty vs midcap $(-26, 2, 24)$: $\cos\theta = 1000 / (28.3 \times 35.4) \approx 0.998$.

The big picture

Two perpendicular pairs

$$ C(A^T) \perp N(A) \qquad C(A) \perp N(A^T) $$

$Ax = 0$ means $x$ is perpendicular to every row, so to the whole row space.

Dimensions add up: $r + (n - r) = n$, $r + (m - r) = m$. Each is the other's orthogonal complement.

With funds

Nifty, bonds, balanced

Do-nothing trade $(-0.5, -0.5, 1)$ ⊥ every rule: return $-6 - 3.5 + 9.5 = 0$.

Reachable targets ⊥ hidden link $(7, -1, 5)$:

Nifty $7 - 12 + 5 = 0$, bonds $7 - 7 + 0 = 0$.

So

A target is reachable ⇔ it is perpendicular to $(7, -1, 5)$.

AᵀA

Invertible ⇔ independent columns

$N(A^TA) = N(A)$, since $x^TA^TAx = \|Ax\|^2$.

Nifty + bonds: $A^TA = \left[\begin{smallmatrix} 146 & 85 \\ 85 & 50 \end{smallmatrix}\right]$, det 75: invertible.

Nifty + twin: $\left[\begin{smallmatrix} 146 & 146 \\ 146 & 146 \end{smallmatrix}\right]$, det 0: singular.

Fredholm

A certificate of "impossible"

Either $Ax = b$ has a solution, or some $y$ has $A^Ty = 0$ and $y^Tb = 1$.

Spread loop $y = (1, 1, -1)$, quotes $(100, 150, 240)$: $A^Ty = 0$, $y^Tb = 10$.

Proof the quotes can't come from real prices: an arbitrage.

Recap

Key takeaways

  1. Orthogonal ⇔ $x^Ty = 0$.
  2. Correlation = cosine of demeaned returns.
  3. Row space ⊥ null space; column space ⊥ left null space.
  4. $A^TA$ invertible ⇔ independent columns.
  5. Fredholm: an impossibility certificate.
