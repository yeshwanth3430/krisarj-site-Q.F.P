Option Pricing Models

What an option is worth, and why.

What this section will cover

Every pricing model in use, in learning order: no-arbitrage and trees, the Black–Scholes family, jumps, local and stochastic volatility, heavy tails and rough vol, American-exercise models, the numerical engines (Monte Carlo, finite differences, Fourier), and the rates models behind bond and LIBOR options.

Why pricing comes first

Every strategy in the next section is judged against fair value. Until you can price an option, you can't know whether a spread is cheap, rich or a coin flip.
